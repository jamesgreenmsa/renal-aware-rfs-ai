Section 1

1The Clinical Problem

Refeeding syndrome (RFS) is a potentially life-threatening metabolic complication of nutritional repletion after a period of caloric restriction. Its biochemical hallmark is acute electrolyte depletion, particularly hypophosphatemia, hypokalemia, and hypomagnesemia, occurring in the setting of carbohydrate reintroduction. In the ICU, however, the clinical picture is routinely more complicated.

🍽️

When does the caloric clock start?

Standard RFS surveillance anchors electrolyte monitoring to formal nutrition orders: enteral or parenteral nutrition initiation. But many ICU patients receive material quantities of intravenous dextrose, dextrose-containing fluids, or propofol (which carries 1.1 kcal/mL as a 10% lipid emulsion) well before a formal nutrition order is placed. If the metabolic clock starts with IV dextrose exposure, the conventional anchor systematically lags behind the actual physiological event.

⏱️

The timing gap

In preliminary MIMIC-IV work, the median interval from last known-normal electrolyte to first observed low was 12.9 hours. Among patients who developed a qualifying deficiency before formal nutrition began, the median interval from that first low to EN/PN initiation was 35.8 hours. The conventional monitoring window, anchored to the nutrition order, would have been open for zero of those 35.8 hours.

🫘

Kidney function changes the picture

Acute kidney injury (AKI) is common in the ICU and directly alters electrolyte handling. Phosphate, potassium, and magnesium are renally excreted; AKI impairs clearance and can simultaneously cause retention and abnormal distribution. Electrolyte deficiencies in a patient with concurrent AKI may reflect nutritional depletion, impaired renal handling, or both. These explanations are not mutually exclusive, and distinguishing them requires integrating renal function trajectories, not just electrolyte values.

📋

Documentation gaps compound the problem

AKI is both under-recognized and under-documented in clinical records. In a separate surgical ICU cohort, approximately 74.3% of creatinine-defined AKI events lacked any AKI mention in the discharge summary. A surveillance system that relies on coded diagnoses or clinician-authored problem lists to identify renal context may therefore be systematically blind to one of the most important confounders in electrolyte interpretation.

The core research question

Can time-aligned consideration of nutritional exposure timing, serial electrolyte trajectories, and longitudinal renal context improve agreement with independent clinical adjudication, better recognition of alternative explanations, and more appropriate reporting of uncertainty compared with conventional laboratory and timing-based surveillance alone?

Section 3  ·  Proposed method

3Candidate AI Architecture

The pilot would develop a modular, auditable evidence integration framework. The architecture is conceptual: no functioning system exists. An interpretation layer could use rules, machine learning, or a combination, subject to evaluation against an independent clinical reference standard.

🍽️

Nutrition module

IV dextrose exposure, timing, and quantity. EN/PN order type, timing, and rate. Caloric anchor assignment.

📉

Electrolyte module

Serial phosphate, potassium, magnesium. Trajectory features: rate of decline, nadir, recovery pattern, lab frequency.

🫘

Renal context module

Serial creatinine, AKI stage, CKD history, renal replacement therapy. Longitudinal trajectory, not point-in-time values.

↓

AI-assisted evidence integration layer

Temporal alignment Aligns evidence streams on a shared time axis anchored to clinical events.
Signal concordance Identifies where nutrition, electrolyte, and renal evidence agree or conflict.
Competing explanations Represents multiple plausible attributions; does not force a single classification.
Uncertainty flagging Flags cases where evidence is insufficient for confident attribution.
Source provenance Audit trail linking each output to the structured data points that support it.
Interpretability Clinician-readable summary; no black-box scores without supporting evidence.
↓

Output: patient-level evidence summary

For each reviewed case: an auditable, time-aligned summary of nutritional exposure, electrolyte trajectory, and renal context; a classification of evidence concordance (concordant / conflicting / indeterminate); candidate explanations with supporting evidence; and an uncertainty flag where applicable. Designed for comparison against independent clinical adjudication, not for direct clinical use.

Comparison: rules-based baseline vs. learned integration

The pilot will contrast two approaches to evidence integration.

Transparent rules baseline

  • Explicit if-then logic applied to each evidence stream
  • Predefined thresholds for electrolyte deficit, AKI stage, and timing windows
  • Fully auditable; requires no training data
  • May not capture complex interactions or trajectory patterns

Learned integration (research option)

  • Model trained to predict clinical adjudicator agreement
  • Can learn trajectory patterns not captured by predefined rules
  • Requires labeled reference cases and rigorous validation
  • Interpretability depends on model choice and explainability tooling

The choice between these approaches, and any combination thereof, is an open design question subject to sample size, label availability, and collaborator input.

Section 5  ·  Proposed method    Open design questions

5Pilot Deliverables and Open Decisions

The LOI maps to three coordinated aims. The primary vehicle for proof-of-concept is MIMIC-IV; the institutional replication target is the Rutgers Clinical Research Data Warehouse (CRDW), with feasibility assessment as an explicit Aim 1 deliverable. The pilot does not promise clinical deployment.

1

Incident electrolyte deficiency prediction model

Develop and validate a model using structured EHR features available at or before nutrition order initiation. Building on MIMIC-IV feature engineering (6,843 admissions, 54.2% RFS prevalence). Prediction time point defined prospectively; CRDW data completeness for IV dextrose, serial electrolytes, and renal function assessed as an explicit feasibility deliverable.

Deliverable: Validated prediction model + CRDW feasibility assessment
2

AKI surveillance and documentation gap characterization

Characterize AKI incidence, trajectory, and discharge-summary documentation concordance in the nutrition surveillance cohort. Compare creatinine-defined AKI events against coded diagnoses and clinical notes. Assess whether renal context, including AKI predictors from the literature, adds incremental signal to the prediction model.

Deliverable: AKI documentation gap manuscript + model feature contribution analysis
3

Renal-aware evidence convergence prototype

Integrate nutritional exposure timing, serial electrolyte trajectories, Aim 1 prediction outputs, and longitudinal renal context into an auditable patient-level interpretation framework. Compare integrated framework against conventional nutrition and laboratory-based assessment using retrospectively reviewed MIMIC-IV cases. Evaluate agreement, uncertainty recognition, and interpretability against independent clinical adjudication. Deliverable is an offline proof of concept, not a clinical tool.

Deliverable: Validated offline convergence prototype + R21-ready plan

Open collaborator decisions

The following design questions require input from Laura and the full team before the protocol is finalized.

  • ?
    Primary clinical use case Which clinical decision does Laura most want the prototype to support: active screening (alert to early depletion), interpretation support (what explains this deficiency?), escalation flagging (which patients need closer monitoring?), or discharge communication (documentation of unrecognized events)?
  • ?
    Adjudicability criteria Which evidence streams are mandatory for a case to be considered adjudicable by the independent clinical reference? Which competing explanations must remain separable (rather than co-attributed) in the framework output?
  • ?
    Primary validation outcome What is the reference standard against which the prototype will be evaluated? Options include structured chart review by a blinded adjudicator, nephrologist review for the AKI component, or a consensus panel process. Agreement metric and acceptable kappa threshold need to be pre-specified.
  • ?
    CRDW feasibility scope Which CRDW elements can be feasibly extracted in the pilot period? Priority candidates are IV dextrose administration records, EN/PN orders, serial electrolytes with collection timestamps, and serial creatinine. Which will be tested first and what constitutes an acceptable completeness threshold?
  • ?
    AI methods justified by sample size Which machine learning or AI approaches are appropriate given the available MIMIC-IV sample size, the label quality from retrospective adjudication, and the staff resources available for this pilot? Rules-based, gradient boosted, and sequence-based approaches each have different data and expertise requirements.
R21 Path

The pilot is designed to generate feasibility evidence for an R21 submission targeting expanded prospective validation and, if warranted, clinical testing. The pilot deliverables are a proof of concept and a feasibility assessment, not a deployable clinical tool. Platform or deployment decisions are out of scope for this award.
